## Chapter/concept map

### Part I. Game Design Basics

- Chapter 1, “The Role of the Game Designer,” defines the designer through advocacy for the player experience and establishes playcentric design as repeated involvement of players throughout development. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 2, “The Structure of Games,” introduces players, objectives, procedures, rules, resources, conflict, boundaries, and outcomes as interacting parts of a designed system. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 3, “Working with Formal Elements,” turns those structural parts into design variables and asks how each one shapes available choices and outcomes. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 4, “Working with Dramatic Elements,” covers challenge, play, premise, character, story, and world as means of creating emotional engagement around formal play. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 5, “Working with System Dynamics,” examines systems, objects, properties, behaviors, relationships, economies, emergence, and feedback as runtime structures. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)

### Part II. Designing a Game

- Chapter 6, “Conceptualization,” moves from goals and constraints through brainstorming, selection, and a concise articulation of the core concept. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 7, “Prototyping,” uses low-cost physical models to test rules and relationships before production investment makes change expensive. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 8, “Digital Prototyping,” distinguishes questions that require software from those better answered on paper and treats digital prototypes as disposable learning instruments. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 9, “Playtesting,” formalizes recruiting, facilitation, observation, data collection, debriefing, and revision while warning designers not to explain away player behavior. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 10, “Functionality, Completeness, and Balance,” progresses from a functioning core through a complete system to tuning relationships, pacing, economies, and fairness. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 11, “Fun and Accessibility,” considers usability, onboarding, challenge, audience difference, and inclusive access as properties of the experience rather than after-market polish. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)

### Part III. Working as a Game Designer

- Chapter 12, “Team Structures,” maps roles, collaboration patterns, authority, and communication across multidisciplinary teams. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 13, “Stages and Methods of Development,” connects concept, preproduction, production, testing, and completion to iterative and agile working methods. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 14, “Communicating Your Designs,” treats documents, presentations, prototypes, diagrams, and conversation as audience-specific design tools. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 15, “Understanding the New Game Industry,” surveys changing platforms, markets, business structures, independent practice, and alternative game contexts. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 16, “Selling Yourself and Your Ideas to the Game Industry,” covers portfolios, pitches, professional positioning, and the ability to show process rather than ideas alone. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)

## Playcentric loop

1. Set player-experience goals before choosing final implementation details. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
2. Build the cheapest prototype that can expose the current design risk. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
3. Observe representative players, including behavior that conflicts with designer intent. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)
4. Revise the system and repeat until the experienced play aligns more closely with the goal. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed summary.)

## Canonical contribution

- Fullerton’s distinctive contribution is not merely “playtest often” but an end-to-end production stance in which player evidence repeatedly arbitrates between design intention and actual experience. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed synthesis.)
- The book connects formal analysis, dramatic design, system dynamics, prototyping, testing, and professional production in one teachable workshop sequence. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; reconstructed synthesis.)
