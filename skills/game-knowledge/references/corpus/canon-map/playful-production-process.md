## Phase map

### Phase One: Ideation—Making Ideas

- Chapters 1–3 begin with blue-sky methods and disciplined research—lists, automatism, images, libraries, field trips, interviews, shadowing, and notes—so ideation alternates divergence with contact with reality. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapters 4–5 distinguish prototypes by purpose and medium, recommend building a digital prototype as a toy before a game, and include sound early when it is structurally relevant. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 6 treats communication, collaboration, leadership, conflict, respect, trust, and consent as design skills rather than management overhead. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 7 separates **experience goals**—what the player should undergo—from **design goals**—what the team will build to support it—and combines them as project goals. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 8 closes ideation with prototype builds, project goals, and accumulated research rather than a prematurely fixed production plan. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)

### Phase Two: Preproduction—Designing by Doing

- Chapter 9 rejects assembly-line certainty for novel design work and frames preproduction as the period in which uncertainty is deliberately reduced through making. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapters 10–11 define and build a vertical slice around the core loop, the “three Cs,” sample levels, blockmesh, committed technology, timeboxing, good housekeeping, debug support, and project goals. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 12 grounds playtesting in the gap among the designer’s model, the system image, and the user’s model, using affordances, signifiers, regular tests, and structured feedback. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 13 introduces **concentric development**: complete and polish primary mechanics before expanding through secondary and tertiary layers, preserving a stable playable center. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapters 14–15 make the vertical slice the preproduction deliverable and reject crunch as a legitimate mechanism for resolving unbounded scope or uncertain design. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 16 maps story structures and emotional beats to game structures while treating story and gameplay as nested, fractal organizations. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapters 17–18 define the **game design macro**: an overview plus a chart sequencing player goals, design goals, and emotional beats across the whole game before micro design. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapters 19–20 connect person-hours, simple schedules, burndown charts, scope cuts, milestone reviews, useful notes, stakeholder presentation, and emotional safety. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 21 makes preproduction a genuine gate: the team commits, changes direction, or cancels based on what the vertical slice and macro revealed. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)

### Phase Three: Full Production—Building and Discovering

- Chapter 22 changes the operating mode from high-uncertainty discovery toward executing a validated task structure while still allowing bounded risks and discoveries. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapters 23–26 distinguish informal playtesting, design-process tests, QA, automation, public tests, formal playtests, interviews, surveys, and consent-aware telemetry. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapters 27–28 connect bug tracking to the alpha milestone, defined through feature completeness, sequence completeness, and a credible onboarding experience. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 29 uses stubs to represent incomplete content or objects so the whole sequence can become testable before final assets exist. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 30 integrates audience development, press, web presence, social media, influencers, and professional marketing with development rather than postponing discoverability until release. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 31 defines beta through content completeness and game health, with remaining work dominated by fixing, tuning, and finish rather than new feature discovery. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)

### Phase Four: Postproduction—Fixing and Polishing

- Chapter 32 organizes bug fixing, polishing, balancing, viewpoint shifts, and waves of postproduction work after the design is substantially complete. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapters 33–34 define release candidate, gold master, release, certification, ratings, and updates as production states with external platform constraints. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 35 acknowledges unexpected design work that appears late and requires classification rather than reflexive acceptance. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 36 closes with release, post-project review, rest, post-project emotions, R&D, departure decisions, and a return to ideation. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)

## Milestone logic

- **End of ideation:** experience/design goals, research, and promising prototype builds exist. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- **End of preproduction:** a representative vertical slice and whole-game macro demonstrate feasibility, quality target, scope knowledge, and production direction. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- **Alpha:** features and full sequence exist in playable form, with onboarding sufficiently real to test. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- **Beta:** content is complete enough that the project can focus on defects, balance, and polish. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)
- **Release candidate:** the build is a plausible ship candidate subject to final verification and certification. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed summary.)

## Canonical contribution

- Lemarchand’s core production theory is that uncertainty should be retired in phase-appropriate ways: prototypes test the idea, a vertical slice tests the production recipe, a macro tests whole-game structure, and milestones progressively restrict admissible change. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed synthesis.)
- Concentric development offers a concrete alternative to broad, shallow implementation: preserve a polished, integrated core and expand outward without allowing every discipline to race ahead independently. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; reconstructed synthesis.)
