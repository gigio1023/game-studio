## Chapter/concept map

### Definition and model

- Chapter 1 defines game feel as real-time control of virtual objects in simulated space, with interactions emphasized through polish, while also examining the broader sensation of control and embodied involvement. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 2 connects feel to perception: players do not directly experience code variables but infer motion, mass, causality, responsiveness, and space from multimodal signals. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 3 models interactivity as a loop among player, physical input device, computer processing, and perceptible output. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 4 separates the mechanics that transform input into motion and state change from the presentation that makes those changes legible and satisfying. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 5, “Beyond Intuition,” argues that feel can be described, measured, compared, and deliberately tuned rather than left to ineffable taste. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)

### Metric families

- Chapter 6, “Input Metrics,” examines sensitivity, dead zones, mappings, combinations, timing, device properties, and the structure of player input. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 7, “Response Metrics,” examines latency, acceleration, deceleration, velocity, displacement, rotation, and the mapping from input magnitude or duration to virtual movement. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 8, “Context Metrics,” locates the controlled object within collision, camera, level geometry, obstacles, scale, and the other constraints that make identical movement parameters feel different. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 9, “Polish Metrics,” covers animation, particles, camera motion, sound, screen effects, deformation, and ancillary feedback that clarify and amplify interaction. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 10, “Metaphor Metrics,” asks what physical or fictional thing the controlled object is understood to be and whether its behavior coheres with that metaphor. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 11, “Rules Metrics,” connects immediate sensation to goals, constraints, rewards, penalties, and strategic consequence; feel without a game context can remain only a toy. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)

### Case studies and principles

- Chapters 12–16 analyze *Asteroids*, *Super Mario Bros.*, *Bionic Commando*, *Super Mario 64*, and *Raptor Safari* as different historical solutions to input, response, context, polish, metaphor, and rule coupling. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 17 distills principles of responsive control, predictable causality, learnable nuance, coherent metaphor, informative polish, and context-sensitive tuning. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 18 uses proposed game concepts to show that feel is a generative design starting point, not only a post hoc polishing discipline. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 19 projects game feel beyond conventional controllers toward new interfaces, sensors, physics, and forms of virtual embodiment. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)

## Six-component diagnostic

The book's practical model can be read as six coupled components; changing any one changes the perceived feel of the whole. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed synthesis.)

1. **Input:** what the player physically does and how it is sampled. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
2. **Response:** how the controlled state changes over time. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
3. **Context:** the space, camera, collision, and situations in which response occurs. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
4. **Polish:** reinforcing sensory signals that expose and exaggerate causality. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
5. **Metaphor:** the identity and expected physical logic of the controlled object. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)
6. **Rules:** why the controlled action matters within goals and consequences. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed summary.)

## Canonical contribution

- Swink makes latency, acceleration curves, camera behavior, collision, audio, particles, and metaphor part of one perceptual design problem rather than separate engineering and art tasks. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed synthesis.)
- “More polish” cannot repair incoherent response or context; polish is multiplicative when it reveals a strong causal loop and distracting when it obscures a weak one. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; reconstructed synthesis.)
