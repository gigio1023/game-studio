# Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design

## Bibliographic record

*Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design* is by Ernest Adams and Joris Dormans, published in June 2012 by New Riders, ISBN 9780321820273. (*Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design*, Ernest Adams and Joris Dormans, 2012.)

## Chapter/concept map

- Chapter 1, “Designing Game Mechanics,” distinguishes rules and mechanics, discrete and continuous systems, and several prototype forms, arguing that mechanics should be explored early rather than left to implementation. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 2, “Emergence and Progression,” contrasts games whose variety arises from interacting rules with games whose authored sequence reveals new situations, then examines hybrid structures. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 3, “Complex Systems and the Structure of Emergence,” connects gameplay emergence to feedback, interdependence, nonlinearity, and state change in complex systems. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 4, “Internal Economy,” describes sources, drains, converters, traders, resources, and feedback structures that move quantities through a game. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 5, “Machinations,” introduces a visual grammar and simulation tool for diagramming resource flows, state changes, triggers, and timing. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 6, “Common Mechanisms,” applies the notation to feedback, randomness, emergence, and recurring mechanisms, including an extended *Pac-Man* model. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 7, “Design Patterns,” turns recurring systemic structures into a pattern language that can be combined, compared, and deliberately altered. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 8, “Simulating and Balancing Games,” uses automated play and models to explore distributions and trajectories, then insists on returning from model behavior to actual game experience. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 9, “Building Economies,” distinguishes games with fixed internal economies from games in which players construct production relationships, using *Caesar III* and the authors’ *Lunar Colony* design as cases. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 10, “Integrating Level Design and Mechanics,” moves from toys to playgrounds, connects missions to spaces, and sequences challenges so levels teach and test the mechanic system. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 11, “Progression Mechanisms,” contrasts authored lock-and-key progression with emergent progression in which access and advancement operate as resources inside the economy. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- Chapter 12, “Meaningful Mechanics,” uses communication theory and semiotics to ask how mechanics and simulations express claims, especially in serious and persuasive games. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)

## Machinations vocabulary

- **Resource connection:** moves a quantitative resource between nodes and can encode rate, probability, interval, or condition. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- **Source and drain:** create and remove resources, defining the open boundaries of an internal economy. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- **Pool:** stores resources and exposes accumulated state. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- **Converter and trader:** transform resource types or exchange resources between inventories. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- **Gate:** distributes flow through deterministic or stochastic routes. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- **State connection and trigger:** allow quantities or events in one part of the diagram to modify behavior elsewhere, enabling feedback and conditional structure. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)

## Design patterns

- The pattern library includes engines that produce or transform resources, friction that removes them, stopping mechanisms, attrition, escalating challenge or complexity, reinforcement of play styles, multiple feedback, trading, worker placement, and slow cycles. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed summary.)
- A pattern is not a complete mechanic or guaranteed solution; it is a relational structure whose player meaning depends on parameters, interface, theme, goals, and other linked patterns. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed synthesis.)

## Canonical contribution

- Adams and Dormans provide the most formal production-facing notation in this corpus, making resource economies executable enough to simulate before full implementation. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed synthesis.)
- The model is intentionally reductive: it is powerful for quantities, flow, timing, and feedback but does not by itself represent embodiment, interpretation, social norms, or audiovisual feel. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; reconstructed critical synthesis.)
