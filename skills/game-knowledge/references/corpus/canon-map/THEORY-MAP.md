## Synthesized causal stack

This stack is **reconstructed/synthesized content**. It combines the corpus into a trace a studio can use; no cited author proposes this exact sequence.

1. **Purpose and responsibility:** state the human or artistic change the project seeks and the harms it will not trade away. (*The Art of Game Design*, Schell, 2019; *A Theory of Fun*, Koster, 2013; synthesized layer.)
2. **Experience goals:** describe what the player should perceive, decide, feel, learn, and do with others. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; *A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; synthesized layer.)
3. **Formal model:** define mechanics, resources, information, goals, uncertainty, and rules that could support those goals. (“MDA,” Hunicke, LeBlanc, and Zubek, 2004; *Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; *Uncertainty in Games*, Costikyan, 2013; synthesized layer.)
4. **Runtime dynamics:** inspect feedback, emergence, strategies, economies, social behavior, and progression generated during play. (*Advanced Game Design*, Sellers, 2017; *Rules of Play*, Salen Tekinbaş and Zimmerman, 2003; synthesized layer.)
5. **Sensorimotor encounter:** tune input, latency, response curves, camera, context, polish, metaphor, and rules as one feel loop. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; synthesized layer.)
6. **Cognitive encounter:** support perception, attention, working memory, mental models, learning, and interpretable feedback. (*The Gamer's Brain*, Hodent, 2017; *A Theory of Fun*, Koster, 2013; synthesized layer.)
7. **Motivational relation:** observe autonomy, competence, relatedness, challenge-skill relation, and the player’s chosen reasons to continue. (“The Motivational Pull of Video Games,” Richard M. Ryan, C. Scott Rigby, and Andrew Przybylski, 2006; *Flow*, Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; synthesized layer.)
8. **Meaning in play:** analyze narrative architecture, procedural claims, performance, appropriation, and cultural context. (“Game Design as Narrative Architecture,” Jenkins, 2004; *Persuasive Games*, Bogost, 2007; “Against Procedurality,” Sicart, 2011; synthesized layer.)
9. **Evidence loop:** compare intended experience with observed behavior, self-report, analytics, and unexpected interpretations. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; *The Gamer's Brain*, Hodent, 2017; synthesized layer.)
10. **Production gate:** retire uncertainty with the cheapest appropriate artifact—prototype, vertical slice, macro, alpha, beta, or release candidate—without transferring unbounded risk into crunch. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; *Blood, Sweat, and Pixels*, Schreier, 2017; synthesized layer.)

## Major bridges

### Meaningful play ↔ MDA ↔ playtesting

- Meaningful play supplies a normative criterion—discernible and integrated action–outcome relationships—while MDA explains a causal route from implemented mechanics to experienced qualities. (*Rules of Play*, Salen Tekinbaş and Zimmerman, 2003; “MDA,” Hunicke, LeBlanc, and Zubek, 2004; synthesized bridge.)
- Fullerton closes the epistemic gap: observed play determines whether the anticipated dynamics and meaning actually appeared. (*Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; synthesized bridge.)

### Systems ↔ economies ↔ progression

- Sellers describes the whole as nested feedback systems; Adams and Dormans provide a notation for quantitative flows inside that whole; Elias, Garfield, and Gutschera expose the experiential consequences of snowballing, catch-up, complexity growth, and collapse. (*Advanced Game Design*, Sellers, 2017; *Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; *Characteristics of Games*, Elias, Garfield, and Gutschera, 2012; synthesized bridge.)
- The practical rule is to simulate economy trajectories, then playtest the strategies, pacing, and emotions those trajectories generate. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; *Advanced Game Design*, Sellers, 2017; synthesized bridge.)

### Uncertainty ↔ flow ↔ competence ↔ learning

- Costikyan identifies what remains unknown; flow asks whether challenge is viable relative to skill; SDT asks whether effective action supports competence; Koster asks what pattern is being learned and when mastery makes it stale. (*Uncertainty in Games*, Costikyan, 2013; *Flow*, Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; “A Motivational Model of Video Game Engagement,” Przybylski, Rigby, and Ryan, 2010; *A Theory of Fun*, Koster, 2013; synthesized bridge.)
- A well-tuned challenge therefore needs an unresolved outcome, learnable causality, reliable control, informative feedback, and room for skill growth; difficulty alone supplies none of these guarantees. (*Uncertainty in Games*, Costikyan, 2013; *Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; *The Gamer's Brain*, Hodent, 2017; synthesized bridge.)

### Feel ↔ usability ↔ intended difficulty

- Swink locates the sensorimotor causes of control quality, while Hodent distinguishes unintended cognitive friction from the intended challenge of the game. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; *The Gamer's Brain*, Hodent, 2017; synthesized bridge.)
- Removing input ambiguity, unreadable feedback, or memory burden need not reduce strategic or performative difficulty; it can expose that difficulty more honestly. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; *The Gamer's Brain*, Hodent, 2017; synthesized bridge.)

### Narrative ↔ simulation ↔ rhetoric

- Juul and Frasca distinguish configured simulation from authored narrative sequence; Jenkins shows how space can organize story possibilities; Bogost shows how executed causal models can make claims. (“Games Telling Stories?,” Juul, 2001; “Simulation versus Narrative,” Frasca, 2003; “Game Design as Narrative Architecture,” Jenkins, 2004; *Persuasive Games*, Bogost, 2007; synthesized bridge.)
- Sicart’s critique prevents the bridge from becoming designer determinism: the player can misunderstand, resist, repurpose, or socially transform the encoded model. (“Against Procedurality,” Sicart, 2011; synthesized bridge.)

### Iteration ↔ gates ↔ labor

- Schell and Fullerton maximize learning through rapid prototypes and player evidence; Lemarchand changes the permitted kind of learning across phases; Schreier demonstrates the human cost when late uncertainty, direction change, and fixed commitments collide. (*The Art of Game Design*, Schell, 2019; *Game Design Workshop*, Fullerton, 2018; *A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; *Blood, Sweat, and Pixels*, Schreier, 2017; synthesized bridge.)
- Iteration is therefore not permission for endless change: early iteration should be cheap and expansive, while later changes require evidence proportional to their downstream cost. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; *Blood, Sweat, and Pixels*, Schreier, 2017; synthesized bridge.)

## Productive tensions

| Tension | Keep both sides |
| --- | --- |
| Formal system vs. lived play | Rules generate affordances and incentives, but player interpretation, skill, identity, and social appropriation complete the event. (*Rules of Play*, Salen Tekinbaş and Zimmerman, 2003; “Against Procedurality,” Sicart, 2011; synthesized tension.) |
| Emergence vs. authored progression | Emergence provides recombinatory depth; progression provides pacing, teaching, revelation, and authored sequence; hybrids deliberately assign different jobs to each. (*Game Mechanics*, Adams and Dormans, 2012; synthesized tension.) |
| Flow vs. interruption | Absorption is valuable for mastery but not mandatory for every aesthetic; reflection, horror, comedy, and social negotiation may require discontinuity. (*Flow*, Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; *The Art of Game Design*, Schell, 2019; synthesized tension.) |
| Balance vs. asymmetry | Fairness can mean equal starts, compensated difference, rotated roles, or equal opportunity to exploit distinct strengths. (*Characteristics of Games*, Elias, Garfield, and Gutschera, 2012; *The Art of Game Design*, Schell, 2019; synthesized tension.) |
| Rewards vs. autonomy | Rewards can inform, celebrate, or structure progress, but controlling rewards can displace self-endorsed reasons for play. (“Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivations,” Richard M. Ryan and Edward L. Deci, 2000; synthesized tension.) |
| Polish vs. causality | Sensory amplification strengthens readable interaction but cannot substitute for coherent response, context, or consequence. (*Game Feel*, Swink, 2008; synthesized tension.) |
| Creative ambition vs. production health | Ambition drives novel work, but quality achieved through hidden contingency and chronic overwork is not evidence of a sound process. (*A Playful Production Process*, Lemarchand, 2021; *Blood, Sweat, and Pixels*, Schreier, 2017; synthesized tension.) |
