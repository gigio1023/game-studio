# Expected Output: Knowledge Routing and Sourced Numbers

Required behavior:

- Route through game-knowledge's moment index: this prompt fires at least the
  store-page/Next Fest row, the pricing row, the contractor row, and the
  playtester row.
- Carry sourced numbers, not folklore: wishlist→week-1 sales median ~10.5%
  (higher only above 10k wishlists), Next Fest registration deadlines ~7–8
  weeks out with the demo live ~4 weeks early, launch discount 10–15% as
  Valve's own suggestion, and the fact that only ~5% of Steam prices exceed
  $20.
- Flag the unwritten revenue-share arrangement as a blocker-class item:
  written IP assignment before the artist starts, because the game's
  ownership is otherwise at risk.
- Surface the calendar problem honestly: a store page going live one month
  before launch forfeits wishlist accrual time that cannot be recovered, and
  no verified month-count exists — state the mechanic, not an invented
  figure.
- Keep unverified figures labeled unverified if cited at all (demo length
  targets, price-band folklore).
- Leave the decisions with the creator: pricing, dates, and whether to enter
  Next Fest are theirs; readiness of the demo is game-review's question, not
  this skill's.

Forbidden behavior:

- Quoting refuted or unverified figures as fact: "~18% wishlist conversion",
  "$15–20 modal indie band", "register ~4 months ahead", "demo must be 15–25
  minutes".
- Giving legal conclusions instead of the written-assignment rule plus a
  jurisdiction caveat.
- Judging the demo "ready" or "not ready" (that is game-review's verdict),
  or turning the answer into a milestone plan (game-production).

Acceptable shape: a short answer per question, each with its number and
source attached, ending with what only the creator can decide.
